Friedensreich Hundertwasser, A Visionary of Ecological Design
Keywords: green architecture; sustainability; eco design

Abstract
In 1908 Vienna has been the capital from which the modern architecture movement in Europe had been initiated. Adolf Loos` essay „Ornament und Verbrechen“ (Ornament and Crime) called the avant- gardists to a more „civilised“ and cultured method of architectural design, beyond every ornament and decoration. Loos wanted even the streets to glow like white walls.
In 1968 Vienna becomes again the stage for an architectural appeal. A naked painter stands up and shouts: „Los vom Loos“ meaning „Loose Loos“) representing the voice of human conscience against the over rationalisation of the built environment and destruction of the natural habitat.
After having painted green roofs in early 1950ies, Friedensreich Hundertwasser’s awareness for the built environment arises and he becomes the initiator of a modern-critical movement. His words are strong and critical as his aim is clear. Basing on the idea of a built environment in harmony with the nature Hundertwasser refuses the uncaring, artificial repetition of the modernity while he misses the physical as well as psychological human dimensions within the design parameters. The copied, straight buildings of the modern movement represent for Hundertwasser a lack of creativity and individuality. Architecture as the „third skin“ has lost the relation to nature and the requirements of human being.
Instead of Loos` white streets Hundertwasser suggests, that the streets should belong as horizontal elements to the natural territory. The occupied area by the buildings should be refunded in the form of green roofs and terraces to the nature. Hundertwasser`s ethical projections are enriched by his aesthetic theory.
He means Loos should have replaced the sterile ornament by living growth. The surfaces should not be flat, that the nature as the best painter can animate the facade with the natural rhythm and richness of the varying daily sunlight.
As of 1972 Hundertwasser develops architectural methods basing on his principle: the horizontal belongs to the nature and the vertical belongs to human. That means trees on the roofs and vegetation through the windows. The implementation of nature into the built environment and into the city was his main aim.
Naive as a child and determined as a vanguard artist, Hundertwasser paints, draws, models and builds his architectural ideas until 2000. Even if his built green and wooded roofs dominate todays images of eco- skyscrapers the scale of his visions (like the Hochwiesenhaus-Modell, the tree renter, or the green motorway) for the aimed harmony of natural and built environment still inspire ecological design methods for instance the Bosco Verticale in Milan. Further his aesthetic thoughts may enrich the thoughts of the emerging discipline of architecture psychology.

1. Introduction
Nowadays it is not something special to talk about sustainable architecture. But only thirty years ago, especially after the second world war, where states had to rebuild and develop, appeals against mass production, overrationalisation of cities were not welcome at all. Vienna, famous as the city of the birth of modernism, had to re-establish after the shadow of the last decades and an economic recovery followed according to the marshall-plan. In those years Friedensreich Hundertwasser exhibited as an international known painter in Mailand (1955) and in Paris (1954, 1956) and recognised the worldwide phenomenon of braking bad against the nature, especially in the metropoles. When Hundertwasser started to paint his green roofs in the early 1950ies, he instinctively participated in the flurishing ecology

debate. Aldo Leopold`s „ A Sand Country Almanac“ the Bibel of the ecological movement was published in 1949, one year after Leopold`s death.
The social residential buildings in Vienna, which tried to cover the need for housing for the national and international migration to the capital, helped itself with the most utilitarian, bald, rational method of modern architecture. This kind of environment was unbearable as the third skin in Hundertwasser’s manner.
In 1958, fifty years after Adolf Loos essay Ornament and Crime, Hundertwasser’s awareness for the built environment arose and he became the initiator of a modern-critical movement. His words were strong and critical as his aim was clear. Basing on the idea of a built environment in harmony with the nature Hundertwasser refused the uncaring, artificial repetition of the modernity while he missed the physical as well as psychological human dimensions within the design parameters. The copied, straight buildings of the modern movement represented for Hundertwasser a lack of creativity and individuality. Architecture as the „third skin“ became a mathematically uniform and lost the relation to nature as well as the requirements of human being.

Man`s Five Skins
Hundertwasser defined five skins, which envelop the bio-psyco-social dimensions of human being. The first skin is the natural epidemis, as the physical enclose of the body. The second skin are the clothes, as fundamental elements of humans intervention with nature. As a further stage in this manner the house represents the third skin. The fourth skin is the social environment. The fifth is the planetary skin adressing the psychological and biological dimensions “directly concerned with the fate of the biosphere, the quality of the air we breathe and the state of the earth`s crust, that shelters and feeds us. For a well tempered life enclosed by those five skins, the balance between built and unbuilt (natural) environment becomes very important. For Hundertwasser clothes and houses have had a development in the last centuries, which did not accord to nature and basic requirements of humans any more. The overrationalisation and overhumanization led us to disasters.

Like Aldo Leopold did in the first half of the twentieth century Hundertwasser continued calling to a sincere closeness to nature:
‘Humans make always everything false to themselves and to the nature. Human has seemingly a self-destructive function on earth. The nature of the flora is always assembling. It is our master, in creative as well as ecological meaning.’
The period of the reconstruction after the second World War Vienna became a stage of modern paradigms of growth and progress. Austria was a well-forested native country, one of the rare countries, where the forest grew. But Hundertwasser as an ecologist who denied man`s right to lord it over the nature, thought in a global scale and criticized the loss of forests and the relation to nature worldwide. The limits of growth were arrived and suggested decentralisation, reflection as well as slowing down in harmony with nature for our safety. The disappearance of the nature from the daily life disturbed on the macro scale the ecological balance of the earth and on the micro scale the psychology of human beeing. Hundertwasser was worried about the overrationalised and overhumanised development worldwide and in Vienna. His sensitive observation and philosophical foresight
persuaded him to dispute the mainstream with its conviction in progress and growth. Built and unbuilt environments drift a part incompatible in the future, thus Hundertwasser called to strive with a peace treaty with nature which contains following points:
1. We must learn the language of nature in order to reach an understanding with her.
2. We must give back territories to nature which we have misappropriated and devastated. For example, according to the principle: everything which is horizontal under the open sky belongs to nature, including, for example roofs, roads.
3. Tolerance of spontaneous vegetation.
4. The creation of man and the creation of nature must be re-united. The rupture of these creations has had catastrophic consequences for nature and man.
5. Life in harmony with the laws of nature.
6. We are only the guests of nature and must behave accordingly. Man is the most dangerous pest ever to devastate the earth. Man must put himself behind ecological barriers so the earth can regenerate.
7. Human society must again become a waste-free society. For only he who honours his own waste and re-uses it in a waste-free society transforms death into life and has the right to live on this earth.

1.2 Hundertwasser`s architecture critic
‘Our cities are the concretisations of the hare-brained ideas of criminal architects, for whom the Hippocratic Oath does not apply, which should sate, I refuse to build buildings which are harmful to nature and man.’ - Hundertwasser 1983
Reminding Günther Rophohl’s critic about humanisation of the ecosystem it is worthy to look at Hundertwaser’s ideas about the balance and intervention between built and unbuilt environments. When Hundertwasser talked about nature, he meant the untouched/pure nature and tried to create
concepts using the nature as it is. Already in the beginning of the 1950ies, Hundertwasser painted even if more instinctively, green roofs. But his critic to architecture became louder in the second half of the fifties, so he wrote his Mouldiness Manifesto against the rationalism in architecture (German Titel: Verschimmelungs-manifest gegen den Rrationalismus in der Architektur) in 1958, exact fifty years after Adolf Loos ‘Ornament and Crime’.
Hundertwasser criticised the modern movement with it’s uncreative straight lines and right angles. As an architecture doctor he proposed aesthetical corrections and ecological solutions for a rethought balance between the built and unbuilt environments. His radical and artistic demeanour succeeded in conveying the message and finding supporters. He took the role of architecture doctor in order to heal the sick overrationalised environment.

After his critics Hundertwasser proposed solutions for the modern architecture. The elimination of the straight line, individualisation of the façade and implementing the nature were his basic formulas. On buildings, in which he was engaged as architecture doctor, the straight line was replaced by curves, the standardised windows varied in size and got
individualised frames. His main issue next to the lack of individual character and standardisation was to naturalise the dominant built environment with green elements. Thus Hundertwasser supposed methods of engaging the nature as it is into the daily life within the urban architecture, even in high skyscrapers. The horizontal surfaces, namely the roofs and the terraces, should be returned to the nature. Trees should be implemented into the living area in the form of tree

2.1 Tree Tenant and Window Rights
Hundertwasser searched the contact to nature in different ways. In some writings he declare the windows as the most important elements of architecture, as they do not exclude nature like the walls, but enable the interaction with light and nature by eye contact. In order to avoid the repetition of
standardised windows and re-establish the residents individuality, people should have the right to design a creative frame surrounding their windows. And in order to naturalise the overhumanised standardised facades, Hundertwasser suggested trees planted on eye level, growing out of the windows, the so calles tree-tenants. The idea of the tree tenants was a part of Hundertwassers restoring nature in urban life. The tree-tenants get a place in the flats and pay the rent by cleaning air as well as rainwater, while calming human’s soul with its natural look. Hundertwasser got the chance to present his eological concept by planting twelve tree-tenants on Via Manzoni in Milan during the Triennale di Milano in 1973. This concept became a fix part of his architecture-doctor interventions and has been adapted in numerous buildings in Austria as well as in Germany.

 2.2 Terrace house and Roof Afforestation
In Hundertwassers architecture formula the tree was more important than the built environment and green roofs were definitely the cover of the future.
Already in 1968 he foresaw the necessity for a law to order the green for horizontal roofs: ‘But I am sure: In a few years it will be necessary, to bring out a building law, which orders for each house, for each garage, for each factory, especially for each factory, one to two meters of
earth on the roof. For grassland, forest, Parks, gardens and agriculture with cows.’
In fact 1992, according to a new amendment for the building law in Vienna it was possible to order green roofs for new buildings by law. Since 2003 roof greenings are funded by the municipality of Vienna.
1972 Hundertwasser had the possibility to show his green roofs and terraces in the TV-Show ‘Wünsch dir was’.

He suggested to enlarge the typical social housing in Vienna. The residents should get a green terrace, in the same size of their flat area. The roofs should be replaced by grasslands.
In 1983 he presented his Hochwiesenhaus concept at the International Gardening Expo, Munich July 1983.
And in the same year 1983, as his ideas gained international acceptance, he got the chance to realise his ideas in the form of a vienniese social housing. Between 1983-1985 Hundertwasser built with the architects Josef Krawina and Peter Pelikan the so called Hundertwasserhaus in the Viennese district Landstraße.
Hundertwasser did not deny vertical living, thus his design for the house based on a vertical village concept. Each of the 52 apartments is separated with an individual colour. The project includes a doctor`s practice, a coffee shop and 37 car parking spaces. This project was a mile stone in the career of Hundertwasser as an architect. In spite of the rich green areas and individual artistic facades the Hundertwasserhaus was completed within the usual construction period and within the budget of a publicly financed housing project.
Next to the colourful appearance the ecological aspect, roof gardens, green terraces, winter gardens and tree tenants are the most important parts of the concept. The aim was to build a house which represents a harmony between human, nature and creativity.
90 tons of soils were used to create 16 private and three common green areas in different sizes.
More than 100 percent of building`s footprint has been returned to the nature in the form of green areas. In1985, 250 trees and shrubs were planted.
2.3. Colour in Nature and The Beauty of Old
‘A house must be an alive, organic, unity, which evolves and transforms itself.’ - Friedensreich Hundertwaser
In terms of colour for Hundertwasser the architecture had two possibilities. Either architecture adjusted itself to nature or it created a good contrast, which highlighted the nature as well as the architecture.
Hundertwasser wanted that architecture and nature next to each other enable their accentuations. The modern dreary facades with their plain surfaces and poor colours are replaced by Hundertwasser’s curved facades and multifarious colour combinations. The curved, plane façade displays the natural light- animations properly and allow permanent change of the image. The rich colour combinations engage with the seasonal colours of the trees on the green roofs and the permanent change of sky.
Efthymios Warlamis writes about Hundertwasser`s colours:
“Hundertwasser`s colors, color tones, and color divisions emphasize ... the shape and the forms of his architecture. They help in blending with the local surroundings. They create effects and counter-effects. They ... bind and divide, accentuate and equalize, but above all they underline ... the optical transition. Contrast and graduation are used in such a way as to contrast with the uniformity in function, structure and construction which emotionalize the environment and create a cheerful mood... He molds and forms spaces with color variations which are simultaneously connected to structure and material constants: Plaster with brick, smooth with rough, matte with shiny ...” (2)
According to his accentuation of the natural, Hundertwasser underlined the aesthetical issue of the process. For Hundertwasser the natural process of decomposition of the façade displays nummeraous layers of the beauty. The emerging images are much more beautiful and perfect than human can ever design. Hundertwasser encloses his ideas with an example. ‘An old wall is beautiful.’ (S.66)
2.4 Hundertwasser’s impact on contemporary architecture
At the end of the 1990ies Hundertwasser already designed about 40 buildings, among them the Waldspirale in Darmstadt with 105 Flats and a green Café terrasse on the 12th floor. In each city his buildings obtained international acceptance. In 1973 Hundertwasser presented as an already famous painter his Tree tenant on the Via Manzoni in Milan during the Triennale di Milano. The tree tenant was a solution for the idea of implementing the nature into the urban life. So Hundertwasser also thought about naturalizing the sky scrapers. Even if his ideas were disbelieved in the late seventies, in 2014 the green skyscraper in Hundertwasser`s meaning became reality in Milan. The afforested skyscraper deploys more than 13,000 plants across more than 90 species and including full-sized trees. The Bosco verticale consists of two towers, both for residential function. The 26 and 18 floors high towers house 700 trees up to six meters high.
Elena Giacomello reports technical details of the project, which seem to display the technical development of Hundertwassers ideas of tree
tenant and afforeted roofs: “All the plants take root in containers located on the external side of deep cantilevered terraces, which are accessible from each apartment...From outside, the plants realize an urban vertical reforestation, providing several environmental and microclimate benefits particular to trees` physiology: dust absorption, pollution reduction, Biogenic Volatile Organic Compounds production, carbon sequestering, air temperature mitigation, and air humidity all increase or improve as an effect of evapotranspiration” and summarises the aesthetical aspects as follows: “As result, the envelope of Bosco Verticale is an active interface to the environment, with a special architectural quality. The dynamism of plant life, in fact, is also expressed in the combination of forms and colours that derives from the carefully selected distribution of different species and specimens, which change over the seasons and the years.” (3)
